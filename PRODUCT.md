# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Tend is for people who want a calm companion for the recurring care that keeps them well. They may open the app once or twice a week, often in a quiet mindset: checking what could use attention, marking something tended, or adding a small ritual before it drifts out of memory.

## Product Purpose

Tend tracks recurring everyday care — rest, reflection, connection, and the rest of life when it still needs tending. Its core job is to answer: what parts of my life could use attention now? Success means users can add an item quickly, understand why it surfaced, and act without guilt-driven overdue language.

Tend is not therapy, diagnosis, or a meditation timer. It does not treat mental health conditions. It is a safe place to remember the small things that keep someone well.

## Brand Personality

Calm, honest, useful. Tend should feel like a cozy nature-focused old comic on warm linen: sun from one side, a quiet grass verge, and thought-shaped cards. Warm, clear, and human, with enough structure to be reliable and no pressure mechanics.

## Anti-references

Tend should not look or behave like a kanban board, task manager, gamified habit tracker, alarm dashboard, clinical mental-health product, or corporate productivity suite. Avoid red overdue states, streaks, points, progress rings, dense analytics, nag loops, harsh validation, and marketing gloss. Do not claim to treat, diagnose, or replace care.

## Design Principles

- Awareness, not pressure: surface drift with neutral language and gentle emphasis.
- Fast to scan: make status, type, rhythm, and last tended easy to understand at a glance.
- Sparse musts: reserve stronger treatment for items that truly cannot drift.
- Honest metadata: show why something is appearing instead of hiding the logic.
- Low-friction capture: adding a recurring item should take under a minute.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target accessible product UI with readable contrast, keyboard-operable controls, clear labels, and reduced-motion support. Status must not rely on color alone, and copy should avoid shame, urgency theater, or punitive phrasing.
